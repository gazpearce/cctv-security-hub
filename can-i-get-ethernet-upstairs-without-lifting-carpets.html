Can I Get Ethernet Upstairs Without Lifting Carpets or Floorboards?

Direct Engineer's Answer

Yes. The cleanest trade solution is an exterior solid-copper Cat6 drop clipped behind rainwater downpipes with weatherproof drip loops. Alternatively, MoCA 2.5 adapters turn existing TV aerial coax into 2.5Gbps Ethernet, or ultra-thin Cat6 can run discretely beneath skirting expansion gaps without disturbing floorboards.

If you work from a first-floor bedroom or attic office in a British home, you have likely suffered through buffering video calls and dropped gaming connections. When you ask a general builder or IT person how to fix it, the first thing they suggest is ripping up hallway carpets, notching floor joists, and drilling through plasterboard ceilings.

Nobody wants newly laid carpets pulled up, especially with underlay that never sits flat again. Fortunately, in 25+ years installing networks across Yorkshire stone cottages, Victorian terraces, and modern new-builds, I rarely ever need to lift a floorboard to deliver full Gigabit hardwired speeds upstairs.

Why Powerline Adapters & Wi-Fi Repeaters Let You Down

Before looking at physical cable routes, it helps to understand why the two most common "plug-and-play" fixes almost always disappoint:

The Powerline Trap

Dual RCD Split Consumer Units

In British electrical installations, upstairs and downstairs socket rings are placed on separate residual current devices (RCDs). Powerline high-frequency RF signals cannot cross RCD induction coils cleanly, resulting in massive jitter, latency spikes, and speeds dropping from 500Mbps to under 30Mbps.

The Mesh Repeater Trap

Masonry & Ceiling Attenuation

A Wi-Fi repeater placed upstairs only repeats the weak signal it receives through dense timber joists and plaster. In Yorkshire stone homes with 22-inch solid walls, high-frequency 5GHz and 6GHz Wi-Fi signals lose over 80% of their power before reaching the upstairs landing.

The 3 Non-Invasive Trade Methods We Use

1

The External Masonry Drop (The Engineer's Choice)

Over 75% of the hardwired upstairs lines I install across Yorkshire use an exterior drop. We drill an 8mm pilot hole downstairs behind your router console, exiting cleanly through the mortar line. We run high-density UV-resistant solid copper Cat6 externally, clip it cleanly alongside or behind downpipes, and enter the upstairs bedroom or office directly behind a desk or skirting.

  • Zero mess inside: Carpets, floorboards, and freshly decorated walls remain 100% untouched.
  • Drip loop protection: A teardrop loop prevents rainwater from following the cable into the building.
  • 10Gbps headroom: Using Cat6a cable future-proofs the line for the next 20 years.
2

MoCA 2.5 Over Existing TV Aerial Coax

If your home has existing coaxial TV aerial sockets downstairs and in the upstairs bedroom, you already have a high-frequency shielded copper pipe in place. With MoCA 2.5 (Multimedia over Coax) adapters, we inject Ethernet data into the coaxial cable at up to 2,500Mbps symmetrical throughput.

  • No new holes needed: Operates entirely through existing wall plates and loft cabling.
  • Rock-solid latency: Typical ping under 3ms with zero jitter, vastly superior to powerline plugs.
  • Double-screened quality: Works seamlessly over UK benchmarked WF100 satellite/aerial cable.
3

Skirting Expansion Gap & Coving Routing

In listed cottages or stone properties where external cabling is restricted by planning rules, we route slim solid-copper Cat6 along the 10mm expansion gap between the gripper rod and skirting board, or concealed inside decorative hallway coving up the stairwell.

  • Completely invisible: Tucked safely beneath the edge of the carpet without lifting the main body.
  • Fire-rated trunking: Where exposed around architraves, ultra-slim micro-trunking color-matched to paintwork is used.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do powerline adapters fail between downstairs and upstairs? ▼

Most modern UK consumer units separate upstairs and downstairs socket rings onto different RCD breakers or independent phases. Powerline high-frequency network signals degrade severely when crossing RCD induction coils, causing packet loss and speed drops down to 10% of router capacity.

Is external Cat6 cable safe from UK weather and frost? ▼

Yes, provided pure solid-copper external-grade cable with a UV-stabilised high-density polyethylene (HDPE) black jacket is installed. It remains impervious to rain, ice, and sun degradation for over 25 years when fitted with downward-angled masonry drip loops.

Can I convert existing TV aerial coax cables into Gigabit Ethernet? ▼

Yes. Using MoCA 2.5 (Multimedia over Coax) adapters, existing double-screened WF100 coaxial cabling carries up to 2,500Mbps symmetrical network data without interfering with Freeview TV signals.

How long does a professional external Cat6 cable drop take? ▼

A standard two-storey external Cat6 drop with discrete clipping, weatherproof entry grommets, and RJ45 wall faceplate termination takes between two and three hours to install and Fluke certify.

Will an external Ethernet cable cause damp or water ingress? ▼

No. Professional engineers drill with a slight downward outward pitch and install a teardrop drip loop below the entry point sealed with neutral-cure exterior silicone grommets, preventing moisture from entering the brickwork.
