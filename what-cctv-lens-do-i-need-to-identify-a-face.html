What CCTV Lens Do I Need to Identify a Face?

Direct Answer

To identify an unknown face at 10 metres with a 4MP or 4K camera, you need a 6mm lens. Court-admissible identification requires 250 pixels per metre (px/m). A standard 2.8mm wide-angle lens only identifies faces up to 4 metres away before pixelation occurs.

The most common complaint I hear from homeowners across Yorkshire is: “Gary, someone walked down my driveway last night, but when the police watched the CCTV, the face was just a blurry smudge.”

Almost every off-the-shelf DIY camera kit comes fitted with a 2.8mm ultra-wide lens. Installers fit them because they give a huge 108° viewing angle that captures the whole front garden. But wide angles spread your camera’s pixels across a massive area. By the time someone is standing at your gate 12 metres away, there aren’t enough pixels across their face to identify them.

Understanding DORI: The Police Evidence Standard

The UK security industry benchmarks camera performance using the international standard BS EN 62676-4 (DORI). DORI breaks surveillance down into four distinct levels of detail:

1. Detection (25 px/m)

You can tell a human is present, but you cannot determine clothing details, gender, or vehicle make.

2. Observation (62.5 px/m)

You can see clothing type (e.g. red hoodie vs black jacket) and determine the rough size of an object.

3. Recognition (125 px/m)

Allows you to recognise someone you already know (like a neighbour, delivery driver, or family member).

4. Identification (250 px/m)

Court-admissible quality. Captures sharp facial features and eye detail of an unknown stranger for criminal prosecution.

Gary's Field Advice: The "Overview + Choke Point" Strategy

You should never try to make a single camera do everything. The best professional security design uses a two-camera approach:

  1. Camera 1 (Overview): A 2.8mm wide-angle turret camera mounted high on the soffit to provide situational awareness of your entire front boundary and vehicles.
  2. Camera 2 (Choke Point): A 6mm or 8mm narrow-angle camera focused specifically on the pinch point where anyone walking towards the property must pass (the front gate or driveway bottleneck) to deliver the full 250 px/m facial identification.

Pairing this with a 24/7 ColorVu camera eliminates infrared glare off white masonry and ensures you record genuine colour jackets and car paints even in complete darkness.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can 4K cameras use a 2.8mm lens and still identify faces at distance? +

4K resolution increases the identification range of a 2.8mm lens from roughly 4 metres to about 6 metres. However, past 7 metres, even 4K pixels become too dispersed across the 108° field of view. For 10m to 15m drives, a 4mm or 6mm lens remains necessary.

What happens if I choose a lens that is too narrow (e.g. 12mm)? A 12mm lens creates a tight "telephoto" tunnel. While someone 20 metres away will be razor-sharp, someone walking close to your wall will pass completely under the camera unseen. That is why focal length must match the physical distance.
