### Wi-Fi Mesh & Network Cabling Questions
Q35: Why is my Wi-Fi weak in some rooms or upstairs?
A35: Modern foil-backed insulation, dense concrete floor screeds, and 500mm solid stone walls attenuate radio frequency signals by up to 25dB–30dB per partition, suffocating standard ISP router signals.

Q36: What is the difference between a Wi-Fi repeater and hardwired access points?
A36: Cheap plug-in repeaters cut bandwidth in half and introduce latency because they communicate wirelessly. Hardwired ceiling access points are connected via solid-copper Cat6 cables directly to the router, delivering full gigabit speeds with zero signal drop.

Q37: Can you run data cabling into a home office without lifting carpet and floorboards?
A37: Yes. We utilize discrete exterior UV-stabilized Cat6 runs along mortar lines, discrete loft cavity drops, and under-floor voids to route network cables neatly into home offices with zero wall destruction.

Q38: What is the difference between Cat6 and Cat6a cabling?
A38: Cat6 supports 1Gbps up to 100 meters and 10Gbps up to 55 meters. Cat6a features internal cross-talk shielding and supports full 10Gbps speeds up to 100 meters, making it ideal for future-proof home networks and commercial offices.

Q39: Do you test data points after installation?
A39: Yes. Every network drop is terminated into professional RJ45 Euro-modules and verified with Fluke cable certifiers for wiremap continuity, attenuation, and gigabit throughput.

Q41: Can you install a TV aerial inside the loft instead of on the roof?
A41: Yes. If local signal strength permits and your roof does not feature signal-blocking foil insulation, we can install a high-gain aerial inside the loft space, protecting it permanently from storm damage.

Q42: Can I get Freeview in multiple bedrooms from a single aerial?
A42: Yes. We install shielded, low-noise multi-way distribution amplifiers in the loft, splitting the signal cleanly to 2, 4, 6, or 8 television points without signal loss.

Q43: Can I get Freeview reception if my property is down in a valley behind a hill?
A43: Yes. We utilize specialized high-gain wideband aerials with low-noise masthead pre-amplifiers, aligning precisely to local relay repeaters to bypass major hill shading.

Q44: Can you repair storm-damaged aerials and loose brackets?
A44: Yes. We replace snapped masts, rusted chimney brackets, and frayed coaxial cables, using heavy-duty galvanized steel brackets that withstand extreme UK weather.
